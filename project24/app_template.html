01 Here

A small yellow star, one of many.

Lit here are its nearest neighbours, the stars within twenty-five parsecs. From a point 1.5 million kilometres beyond Earth, the Gaia spacecraft watched each star trace a tiny ellipse on the sky as we circled the Sun. The size of that ellipse is called parallax. From the parallax, a distance. From the distance, a place.

parallax > 10 milliarcseconds ⟷ distance < 100 parsecs

02 Sphere

215,317

Pull back and the neighborhood becomes a sphere two hundred parsecs across, evenly sown. Lit here are the red dwarfs, 73% of everything. They are small and cool, and they burn so slowly that not one has yet died.

red dwarfs: colour BP − RP > 1.8

03 Embers

15,373

White dwarfs. Each is the core a star left behind when it died: the size of Earth, heavier than half a Sun, cooling for billions of years. They are old enough to have wandered everywhere, and so they are everywhere.

white dwarf: absolute G > 10 + 2.5 × (BP − RP)

04 Giants

747

Giants. Stars in old age, the hydrogen at their centres spent, swollen to many times the Sun's size and tens of times its brightness. Rare, and scattered like lanterns across the whole sphere.

giant: absolute G < 3.5 and BP − RP > 1.0

05 Family

The Hyades forty-seven parsecs toward Taurus

A knot of stars born together about 650 million years ago, still travelling as one. The nearest star cluster to the Sun. Here only the brightest stars are lit, in a slice of space twenty-four parsecs thick.

centre x −43.9 · y 0.0 · z −18.1 pc
